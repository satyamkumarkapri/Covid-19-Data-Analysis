M1: Data Preprocessing & Cleaning

Before analyzing data, we must clean it to ensure accuracy. Real-world data is often messy, containing errors or gaps. Here is how our automated pipeline prepares the raw Johns Hopkins dataset:

Missing Values

Days where countries failed to report cases result in missing values (NaN). We resolve this using mathematical interpolation and .fillna(0) to ensure continuous time-series data without gaps.

Duplicate Values

Some countries report data by province, resulting in overlapping rows. We aggregate and merge these using .groupby() to guarantee exactly one clean row per country per day.

Outlier Detection

Sudden massive negative cases often appear due to historical data corrections by governments. We bound our data and clip negative daily spikes to prevent them from skewing our statistical models.

Results and Discussion

Major Observations

Our exploratory data analysis reveals distinct waves of infection globally. The line charts demonstrate how quickly the virus spread during peak months. The bar charts highlight that certain heavily populated or highly connected countries bore the brunt of the absolute case counts. We also observe a strong correlation between confirmed cases and recoveries.

Statistical Findings

Through hypothesis testing (T-Test), we established a statistically significant difference in daily case rates between the first and second halves of the pandemic. Additionally, ANOVA testing confirmed that the variance in case growth among the top three most affected countries is significantly different, indicating different effectiveness in national containment strategies.

Model Performance

The Linear Regression model provided a simplified baseline for forecasting. While the R² score indicates a reasonable fit for the linear trend, the high MAE and RMSE values reflect the inherent difficulty of predicting exponential, highly volatile pandemic growth using purely linear assumptions. Non-linear models would capture these dynamics better.

Recommendations

Based on our findings, we recommend maintaining robust early-warning systems (as visualized by daily surge tracking) and prioritizing vaccination in regions with lower historical recovery rates. The data also underscores the importance of stringent containment measures to flatten the curve and avoid the massive spikes seen in the first year.

Conclusion

Project Summary

We successfully built an automated, end-to-end data pipeline that ingests raw COVID-19 data, cleans it (M1), and extracts actionable insights through EDA (M2), Probability Analysis (M3), Statistical Inference (M4), and Machine Learning (M5) into a unified Dashboard (M6).

Key Insights
  • Recovery rates remain strong despite initial mortality spikes.
  • Statistical variance between nations highlights the impact of diverse public health policies.
Prediction Accuracy
  • The baseline regression model captures the macroscopic upward trajectory but fails to model short-term exponential waves accurately.
  • Future iterations should employ Time-Series models like ARIMA or LSTMs for enhanced predictive accuracy.
